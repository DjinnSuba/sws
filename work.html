What I report on

Four beats, one throughline: how policy decisions made in conference rooms land on the people who never get a seat at the table.

01 Housing Eviction courts, landlord networks, zoning fights, and the slow erosion of affordable stock across Cook County.
02 Labor Warehouse conditions, gig work, union drives, and wage theft investigations built from payroll records and testimony.
03 Public Systems School closures, transit equity, and municipal budgets — tracing where public money actually goes.
04 Data & FOIA Records requests, database building, and computational reporting that turns raw filings into accountability stories.

About

Reporting that shows the work

I got into journalism through public records, not press releases — a habit that still shapes every story I take on. Before joining the Tribune's investigations team, I covered city hall for a nonprofit newsroom and spent two years building FOIA pipelines for reporters across the Midwest.

My stories tend to start with a spreadsheet and end with someone's kitchen table. I care about precision as much as I care about access: every figure in my work is one I can hand to a reader, a lawyer, or an editor and defend line by line.
